### The condition, which is not a footnote

**0.9357 is a statement about how much of Magic these phases *look at*, not about
how much of Magic is modelled.** Of 77,348 clauses in the legal pool, **69,169 —
89.4% — are classified inert**: real rules text, in a currency the simulated
phases do not spend. Combat, removal, counters, the whole stack.

That classification is defensible and it is the one the design asks for. §3
simulates turns 1–4 exactly and asks a single question of the result — can this
deck cast its spells and reach turn 5 functional — so a vanilla 4/4 *is* fully
modelled for that purpose: a cost, a colour requirement, and a body. Calling it
unrepresentable would exclude most of Magic to no benefit and understate the
pool enormously.

But it means the honest reading of the gate is narrower than the headline:

> **The opening-phase model is sound.** For the feasibility gate this sprint
> exists to serve, 94% of the legal pool can be represented. It says nothing yet
> about a model that watches turn nine.

**G1 must be re-measured when E3 widens what is observed**, and the number will
fall — that is expected, not a regression. Inertia is relative to what is being
simulated, and what is being simulated changes. A later sprint that inherits
0.9357 without re-measuring will be quoting an answer to a different question.
This is recorded in the spec beside the figure so it cannot be quoted bare.

### What the classifier nearly got away with

The first measurement read **0.9249**, and it was wrong in a way that would have
survived review. **481 clauses of the form `{T}: Add {W} or {U}.` — dual lands —
were being read as fixed `{W}`.** The model had no way to say "either", so the
classifier silently picked one.

Colour flexibility on turn two is close to the *entire* thing the opening phase
measures. A dual land reported as mono is not a rounding error; it is the
measurement being wrong about the cards the measurement is most about.

The fix was to notice that a choice from a set *printed on the card* is
expressible — the set is right there — and to give it its own opcode. Coverage
moved to 0.9357. The number went up, which is the uncomfortable part: a
correctness fix that raises your headline figure deserves more scepticism than
one that lowers it, and the reason this one is sound is that it moved 856
clauses from "silently wrong" to "correctly described", not from "unmatched" to
"waved through".

Found by asking the data what the classifier accepted, rather than by reading
the classifier. That is now the habit for anything measured against real input.

### What is unmatched, ranked

2,392 clauses across 1,630 distinct shapes — a long tail with one clear head:

| count | shape |
| ----: | ----- |
| 119 | `If you do, draw a card.` |
| 18 | `If you do, draw two cards.` |
| 15 | `Whenever this creature deals combat damage to a player, draw a card.` |
| 13 | `At the beginning of your draw step, ...` |
| 11 | `Whenever you draw your second card each turn, ...` |

**Conditional and triggered draw is the single biggest lever**, and by a wide
margin. Nothing else clusters: past the top ten the tail is essentially unique
shapes, which is the signal that the remaining work is diminishing rather than
blocked. If coverage ever needs to be higher, that is where to spend the effort
— and the list is the deliverable, not the slogan "grow the opcode set".

### Not delivered

- **The 32-byte card struct (T2).** Not started. It constrains a *runtime* card
  that does not exist yet — nothing executes opcodes — and the emitted table is
  the JSONL intermediate 1.3 replaces. Doing it now would mean packing bits
  against an opcode payload that G1's tail is about to change. It moves to 1.3,
  where the binary table is already scheduled and the layout is decided once.
- **`--report-unmatched` (T6).** The ranking above was produced by a script
  against the export, not by `mfsim`. The number that gates the project is in
  the artifact; the list that guides the next sprint is not, and should be.

## Design amendments

To apply with this retro: the G1 result and its condition into
`simulator-spec.yaml` under `validation`, and §13.5 gains the distinction
between *inert* and *unrepresentable* — the design describes coverage without
ever saying what it is coverage *of*, which is exactly the ambiguity that made
this measurement worth arguing about.

## Revised risks

| Risk | Change |
| ---- | ------ |
| **G1 fails and kills the project** | ✅ **Retired at 0.9357** — but replaced, not removed. See the next row |
| **The gate answers a narrower question than it appears to** | ⚠️ New, and the real one. 89.4% of clauses are inert *because the simulated phases are few*. Re-measure at E3. A number quoted without the condition is a number about the wrong question |
| **A classifier agrees with itself** | ⚠️ New. The dual-land error was invisible to every test, because the tests were written from the same reading of the text as the code. Only interrogating the real data found it — the same lesson 1.1 learned about JSONL, arriving a second time in a different disguise |
| **Real uncovered branches** | ↔ Six `\|\|` alternatives in the classifier where an earlier marker always matches first. Above the floor; not chased |

## Carry-over

- **T2, the 32-byte card struct → 1.3**, with a reason: the layout should be
  decided once, against a settled opcode payload, alongside the binary table.
- **T6's unmatched report → 1.3 or wherever coverage is next touched.** Small,
  and the thing that makes "grow the opcode set" actionable.
