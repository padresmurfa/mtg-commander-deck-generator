# Retro — Sprint 3.2 — Objective & sampling

**Epic:** E3 Solo evaluation · **Gates:** none (G4 is 3.3)

## Headline

The sprint set out to fix a metric and found that three of its four measurements point at the same
thing, which is not the metric.

**The objective was chosen and pre-registered**: the total mana value cast in the *aggregate*
phases, commander included, printed cost, lands zero. Three of those clauses are 3.1's score. The
fourth is a correction to it — §3 says the feasibility gate contributes "zero to fitness beyond
pass/fail" and 3.1's score summed the whole run, so it weighted turns 1–4 after all. The design of
record was right and the code had drifted from it.

**The carried-in instruction could not be satisfied as written, and finding out why was most of the
task.** "Choose an objective that is not any policy's own objective" asks for a quantity no rung
optimises, and every quantity the state vector records is some rung's greedy target — `deployed` is
`expensive-first`'s, `spells` is `cheapest-first`'s, `mana` is `ramp-first`'s, `wasted` is the
careful land rule's, `mulligans` is the mulligan policy's. That is not a coincidence to design
around: §5's rungs are greedy rules over exactly what the model can observe, because the rungs and
the vector came from the same model of a turn. **Looking for an objective no rung optimises is
looking for a quantity the model cannot see.**

So the check asks the question the instruction was reaching for, in two parts fixed before
measuring. Does it rank **decks** — what the GA needs? Does it rank **strategies** — what §4's
max-over-plans and G3 need? Reported separately, because only the first is fatal here now that
sprint 5.0 owns the policy gap.

> **Condition 1 passes. Condition 2 fails, and `greedy` is the argmax on every deck.**

Decomposed one policy parameter at a time (2.3's move), the two components have **opposite signs**:

| Deck | greedy's mulligan | greedy's cast rule |
| ---- | ----------------- | ------------------ |
| aggro | **+1.18** | −0.83 |
| midrange | **+0.93** | −0.36 |
| ramp | **+4.39** | −0.17 |
| fixing | **+0.98** | −0.58 |

The worst rung wins, and it wins *despite* its cast rule. And then the same root cause turned up
twice more, in tasks that had nothing to do with each other:

- **T4.** §7.3 names the opening-hand land count as "the dominant variance source" and predicts
  3–5× fewer games from stratifying on it. Measured, it explains **4.8%** of the variance of the
  objective, **7.2%** of the mana entering turn 5 and **3.2%** of the gate's pass rate. The ceiling
  on a perfect stratification is **1.06×**, and end to end the two-phase estimator is a **loss**.
- **T6.** Two of §13.3's four known-bad decks are not caught. Sixty lands scores 0.94 of a real deck
  and clears the feasibility gate **more often than the real deck does**.

Every one of those is the same sentence 3.1 wrote: **a null-opponent model with a fixed horizon
prices position at zero.** A mulligan costs a card and buys an early game worth nothing. Flooding
costs the turns you spent drawing lands and screw costs the turns you spent not casting, and there
is no clock to charge either against. §7.3's stratification does not pay because the *mulligan*
absorbs the variable it conditions on — which is the same clock, seen from the sampler's end.

Nothing was patched to make any of it pass. Replacing a pre-registered metric inside the sprint
that measured it failing is the one move pre-registration forbids, and 3.1 established the
precedent by refusing it.

## Gate validation

**This sprint carried no gate.** G4 is sprint 3.3.

Two findings bear on it directly and are recorded here so 3.3 cannot be read innocently:

- **G4 ranks precons by this objective, and this objective does not catch flood or screw.** Precons
  are built to a sensible land count, so the *specific* failure may not bite — but a rank
  correlation computed with a known blind spot in the metric must be reported with it, not after it.
- **The aggregation error is deck-differential (3.1) and G4 cannot see it.** Unchanged, and now
  joined by a second bias in the same direction.

## Revised risks

**Scarier.**

- **The clock is now three findings deep.** G3 failed on it (3.1), the mulligan term inverts on it
  (T1), and half of §13.3 escapes because of it (T6). It is no longer a limitation of one
  measurement; it is the dominant property of the solo model, and every later number inherits it.
- **G4 is next and is measured against a metric with a known blind spot.** A good Spearman would be
  reassuring and would not clear the blind spot; a bad one would be ambiguous between the metric and
  the model.

**New.**

- **A loose bar is worse than no bar.** T4's first conditional shuffle carried a 0.4-mana bias past
  an agreement test whose bar was wide enough to hold one. The bias was found by asking the
  distribution directly — "the next card is a land with probability (L−h)/92" — rather than by
  comparing means. Prefer a test that names the exact quantity.
- **An explanation offered without a test is a guess.** T4's subset-sum argument for why
  `cheapest-first` wins was clean, plausible and wrong; a hand of `{2, 3}` against a budget of 3
  refutes it. It is now a test so it cannot come back.

**Stopped mattering.**

- **"The solo objective is close to one policy's rule" as a framing.** T1 replaced it with a
  checkable condition. 3.1's version also named the wrong rung.
