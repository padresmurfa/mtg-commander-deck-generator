# Retro — Sprint 3.3.1 — Fixture resolution

**Epic:** E3 Solo evaluation · **Gates:** G4 (re-run)

## Headline

> **G4 failed. ρ = −0.036 over 48 real precons, and the objective's ranking of real decks is
> indistinguishable from chance.**

Sprint 3.3 deferred G4 because the corpus was empty. This sprint made it measurable and the gate
answered — with the answer the plan wrote down in advance as a real possibility:

    corpus 48   scored 48   feasible 15   substituted 570 (233 lands, 13 commanders)
    rho -0.0357   z -0.24   sim_spread 43.28   data_spread 27.05      VERDICT = FAIL

**Thresholds unchanged from 3.3** — ρ ≥ 0.35, z ≥ 2, 2,048 games per deck, n ≥ 100, all committed
before the win-rate data existed in the tree. Only the instrument changed, which is 3.1's rule about
never improving an instrument and loosening a bar in the same breath.

**This is not a noisy measurement of a weak signal.** Across five seeds ρ is −0.036, −0.021, −0.033,
−0.022, −0.027 — a tight cluster about zero. Both sides have room to correlate: 43.3 points of
simulated fitness spread against 27.0 percentage points of win rate. The orderings simply have
nothing to do with each other.

## Gate validation

### G4 — precon rank correlation

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Question** | Does the objective rank real decks in the right order? |
| **Threshold** | ρ ≥ 0.35 **and** ρ·√(N−1) ≥ 2, at 2,048 games per deck, on precons with n ≥ 100 — all fixed in sprint 3.3 before the data existed |
| **Measured** | corpus 48, scored 48, **ρ = −0.0357, z = −0.24** |
| **Card table** | `349888c09657e4361deac00e01c0f21c` — recorded in the record itself (§14.2), because the ρ that follows a §3 redesign is only comparable to this one if both read the same cards |
| **Outcome** | **FAILED** |

**The written failure branch, applied:** *"the objective is wrong. Redesign §3 rather than tuning λ
until the numbers look nice."* λ is untouched, and 3.2 deliberately left it untuned so that it could
not become the knob somebody reached for at exactly this moment.

**Of the three blind spots pre-registered before the measurement, the third is the one that bit.**

| Blind spot | Verdict |
| ---------- | ------- |
| Two of §13.3's known-bad decks uncaught (3.2) | Not implicated — precons carry sensible land counts |
| The aggregation error is deck-differential (3.1) | Still present, still uncorrectable, and 10–20% is far too small to explain ρ ≈ 0 |
| **The objective ranks decks and does not rank strategies** (3.2) | **Confirmed on real decks.** `greedy` wins 38 of 48; `sequencing-aware` — the top of §5's ladder — wins **none** |

**Two diagnostics recorded beside ρ, both already computed inside the per-deck fit and previously
discarded.** They exist because "ordered no better than chance" and "cannot be played at all"
produce the same ρ and have different fixes:

- **feasible 15 of 48.** Two-thirds of real, published, playable Commander decks fail the solo
  feasibility gate. The model is not ranking these decks badly so much as failing to play them.
- **best rung: greedy 38, curve-out 8, role-aware 2, sequencing-aware 0.** §4's fitness is a max over
  admissible plans, and on real decks that max is attained by the simplest plan four times out of
  five and by the most careful one never.

**What must not happen next, written down before anybody measures it.** ρ over the feasible 15 is
the obvious follow-up, and it is a **post-hoc subgroup chosen after seeing a failure** — the move
pre-registration exists to forbid. It is not computed in this sprint. If it becomes a gate it is
pre-registered first, with its own threshold and its own sample size, exactly as 3.3 did for ρ.

## Revised risks

**Scarier.**

- **E4 is blocked by a gate that answered "no".** 3.3 blocked it on a gate that had not answered;
  this is worse. Building a search against an objective measured to rank real decks at chance is
  precisely what the gate was placed early to prevent.
- **The clock, five findings deep.** 3.1 found it in G3 and 3.2 found it three times — the mulligan
  term, §7.3's stratification premise, and flood/screw. This is the fifth sighting and the first
  against data from outside the simulator: a null-opponent model with a fixed
  horizon prices position at zero, so the strategy axis collapses and the fitness measures something
  real decks are not selected on. **5.0 — the mirror match — is now the most load-bearing sprint in
  the plan.**

**New.**

- **The model cannot play two-thirds of real decks.** `feasible 15/48` is not a ranking problem, and
  it was invisible until a diagnostic that costs nothing was finally recorded. Worth asking of every
  gate: what would this number look like if the thing being graded were not working at all?
- **The orchestrator's growth loop cannot help when the arena that is short is the fixed one.** The
  first real G4 run read a 2.9 MB card table into the 1 MB root arena; the orchestrator grew
  `arena_bytes` three times, to the ceiling, and gave up. It grew the wrong knob, correctly and
  uselessly, and the report said nothing about which arena had run out.

**Stopped mattering.**

- **Whether the fixture corpus can be built.** It can: 188 of 190 precons resolve through the
  stand-in, and 48 of 48 corpus rows score. The two that do not are the two banned cards, which is
  the legality filter working.

## Why `current_sprint` is not advanced

`CLAUDE.md` §5: *"If a gate fails, stop and escalate — the failure branch is written down for each
one, and it is a real branch, not a formality."* G4's branch is **redesign §3**, and what that means
in practice — a different objective, an opponent at last, or a narrower claim for the whole project —
is a decision that belongs to whoever owns the plan. Task-splitting a sprint here would be choosing
the answer by choosing the work.
