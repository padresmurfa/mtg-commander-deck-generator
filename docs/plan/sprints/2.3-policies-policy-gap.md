# Sprint 2.3 — Policies and the policy gap

**Epic:** E2 Opening phase · **Gate:** **G3 — policy gap discriminates** · **Status:** `done` · [retro](../retros/2.3-policies-policy-gap.md)

## Goal

The policy ladder from §5, and the gate that asks whether it means anything: does naive-versus-careful
separate decks known to be forgiving from decks known to be demanding?

## Why this next

2.2 built one policy and **built it badly on purpose.** Greedy will not cast a ritual to enable a
bigger spell. It sequences taplands by a flag rather than by whether the mana would actually be used.
Both were recorded at the time as value deliberately left on the table.

That gap is not a defect to fix quietly — §5 says the gap **is** the skill requirement. Measure the
same deck under a deliberately naive policy and under the best available one, and the difference *is*
the number. So the weak baseline is the instrument, not a shortcoming of it.

G3 asks whether the instrument reads anything at all. If the gap is noise, §5 is unfounded and the
bracket system is built on a measurement that does not exist.

## Decisions, settled before the code

### What G3 actually asks — a difference of differences, not a difference

Read literally, the gate is *"does naive-vs-careful separate **known-forgiving from
known-demanding** decks?"* That is not "is the gap non-zero". It is:

> gap(demanding) − gap(forgiving) > threshold

A deck where sequencing cannot matter — mono-colour, all untapped basics, flat cheap curve — must
show **almost no gap**. A deck where it matters enormously — three colours, many taplands, tight
colour requirements — must show a large one. A measurement where both decks show the same healthy
gap would satisfy "the gap is non-zero" and tell us nothing about skill, because it would mean the
gap is a property of the *policies* rather than of the deck.

That framing is strictly harder to pass and it is what the gate says. Fixing it now, before the
fixtures are built, is the only way the fixtures do not get chosen to fit.

### What "performance" is, for a phase that only produces pass/fail

§3 sanctions exactly one output from the opening: the binary feasibility gate. So the **primary
metric is the gate pass rate**, and the gap is a difference of two rates.

**This has a hazard worth naming in advance: the bar is deliberately low.** §3 wants it low so that
weighting opening strength does not breed out every control deck — but a rate near 1.0 has little
room left to move, so a low bar is a *bad instrument* for measuring a policy gap even while being
the right gate for fitness. The 2.2 validate deck passed 30 of 32.

So the finer counters — `mana_wasted`, `spells`, `mana_spent` — are recorded **beside** the rate as
secondary. They are not what §5 means by performance, and they are not the gate. They are there to
tell two failures apart:

| Primary | Secondary | Call |
| --- | --- | --- |
| separates | — | **G3 passes** |
| flat | separates | **G3 deferred** to E3. The signal exists; the instrument is too coarse to see it |
| flat | flat | **G3 fails.** §5 is unfounded |

Writing the three-way branch down in advance is the point. A two-way gate would force "the skill
signal does not exist" onto evidence that only says "a binary pass/fail at a low bar cannot see it".

### The threshold — 5× a noise floor measured first

`SD` is measured over disjoint seed blocks of the same statistic, so it is the sampling variability
of the number actually being graded rather than an estimate of something adjacent. **The multiple is
fixed here at 5**, before the noise floor is a number, for the same reason G2's 5σ was fixed before
the deviation was: a threshold derived from the run it grades is not a threshold.

Five rather than three keeps the constant identical to G2 and T6. Re-deriving it per measurement is
exactly how a constant ends up chosen to fit.

**Ask the vacuous question** (2.1): what would make this pass without a signal? Two answers, both
guarded. A noise floor measured as zero would divide every separation to infinity — so a zero SD
fails rather than passes. And a "careful" policy differing from naive in something *irrelevant*
would produce a real but meaningless gap — which the difference-of-differences framing already
handles, because an irrelevant difference cannot know which deck is demanding.

### Do the rungs differ in their mulligan? Yes — and the gap is decomposed

§4 is explicit: keep/mull judgment is one of the largest skill differentiators, *"a large part of the
measured skill gap comes from exactly here, and factoring it out would erase the signal."*

That is a **testable claim about the design that nothing has tested**, so this sprint tests it. Three
gaps, not one:

- `gap_total` — careful mulligan and careful play, against naive both
- `gap_play` — careful play, naive mulligan, against naive both
- `gap_mull` — careful mulligan, naive play, against naive both

It costs nothing but extra policy rows, and it says whether §4's "large part" is true, roughly true,
or backwards.

### What the opening phase cannot express, of §5's six policies

Recorded now because it bounds what this sprint can honestly claim:

| Policy | In the opening phase |
| --- | --- |
| `greedy` | ✅ 2.2 built it, deliberately badly |
| `curve-out` | ✅ cheapest-first casting |
| `role-aware` | ~ partial. Ramp is observable; removal and interaction are not |
| `sequencing-aware` | ✅ this sprint's careful rung |
| `hold-interaction` | ❌ **meaningless without an opponent.** Nothing to hold up mana *for* |
| `combo-assemble` | ❌ needs combo detection, which nothing builds yet |

So the experienced bracket's admissible set — `{role-aware, sequencing-aware, hold-interaction}` —
has one member that is inert here and one that is partial. G3 is measured between the two ends that
*are* expressible, `greedy` and `sequencing-aware`, and the ladder is built with the others present
and honestly labelled rather than quietly omitted.

## Risks carried in

- **A gate must be unable to pass vacuously** (2.1). Ask it of G3: what measurement would make the
  gap look real without one existing? Two policies that differ in name only would report a gap of
  zero, which fails — but a policy pair that differs in something *irrelevant* would report noise and
  might not.
- **A defect can hide from the state vector** (2.2). It reports the pool after everything untaps, so
  within-turn timing is invisible through it. A careful policy is *entirely* about within-turn
  timing, so this is a sharper risk here than it was there.
- **The metacard carries no amounts** (2.2). Draw and cost reduction both assume one. A policy cannot
  sequence around a number the model does not carry, so this bounds how careful "careful" can be —
  and it qualifies G3 the way it qualifies G1.
- **The bottoming rule is a guess** (2.1), now affecting every game played. It is a policy decision
  and this is the policy sprint.
