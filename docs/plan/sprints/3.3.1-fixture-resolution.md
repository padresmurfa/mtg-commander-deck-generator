# Sprint 3.3.1 — Fixture resolution

**Epic:** E3 Solo evaluation · **Gate:** **G4**, re-run · **Status:** `not_started`

## Goal

Make G4 measurable. Not one of the 190 precons resolves completely against the card table, so the
corpus the gate needs is empty. This sprint decides — **in advance** — what a deck containing an
unrepresentable card *is*, and re-runs G4 with 3.3's thresholds unchanged.

## Why this next, and why not E4

**G4 did not fail. It could not be measured**, and a gate that has not answered is not one that has
been passed. E4 builds the search against the objective G4 exists to validate, and `gates.md` opens
by saying gates go as early as the dependency graph allows *because the point is to be wrong
cheaply*. Starting the search epic first spends exactly that saving.

Inserted on the 0.2.1 / 1.2.1 / 5.0 precedent — a numbered insertion with its reason recorded,
rather than a rewrite of a fixed epic.

## The decision this sprint exists to make

An unrepresentable card is excluded from the card table (§7.9), so a precon's list has holes in it:
best case one, mean 9.3, worst 22, and 19% of precons lose their *commander*. Two ways forward, and
they are not exclusive:

1. **A substitution semantics.** An unrepresentable card still has a **mana cost and a type**, and
   this model is overwhelmingly about mana — so a same-cost vanilla permanent preserves the curve
   exactly and loses only the effect, which the model already cannot see. That is a real argument
   rather than a convenience, and it makes G4's corpus 48 decks instead of none.
2. **A wider opcode set**, which is §13.5's own failure branch and now has a far sharper argument
   for it than 0.9301 ever gave.

**Whichever is chosen, it is written down and committed before anything is measured.** ρ = −0.0137
has already been seen, on patched decks; choosing the patch rule now *in the light of it* is the one
move pre-registration forbids, and 3.1 established the precedent by refusing exactly this.

## Exit criteria

- [ ] The resolution rule **pre-registered and committed before any correlation is computed**, with
      its argument, and with what it is known to distort stated alongside
- [ ] Per-deck completeness reported beside G1's clause fraction, everywhere G1 is quoted
- [ ] **G4 re-run with 3.3's thresholds unchanged** — ρ ≥ 0.35, z ≥ 2, 2,048 games per deck,
      n ≥ 100 — and its verdict recorded as `passed` / `failed` / `deferred`
- [ ] G4 written to the run artifact by `mfsim validate`, so the number comes from the tool as G1's
      and G2's do
- [ ] `make check` green at ≥95% line and branch

## Risks carried in

- **Thresholds must not move.** 3.3 fixed them before the data. Improving the instrument and
  loosening the bar at once makes the result unreadable — 3.1's rule, applied.
- **A fixture that patches itself hides the state of the world** (3.3). The hole-filling resolver
  was three lines, looked defensive, and turned an empty corpus into 35 plausible decks and a
  number. Whatever rule is chosen, the count of substituted cards is reported per deck.
- **Pre-registration protects the threshold and does not make you think of everything** (3.3). Three
  blind spots were named in advance and none of them is why G4 deferred.
- **G1's clause coverage does not bound per-deck completeness** (3.3), and the two were being read
  as though one implied the other.
- **The clock, three findings deep** (3.1, 3.2). G4 is still the first outside check on it, and it
  has not run.
