# Sprint 3.2 — Objective & sampling

**Epic:** E3 Solo evaluation · **Gate:** none (G4 is 3.3) · **Status:** `done` — [retro](../retros/3.2-objective-sampling.md)

## Goal

An objective worth optimising — chosen against the degeneracy 3.1 disclosed — plus CVaR
scalarisation and stratified sampling, so a solo evaluation produces a *fitness* rather than a
number that happened to accumulate.

## Why this next

3.1 built a continuous score and then measured it into the ground. Two things it found land
squarely here:

1. **The score is close to one policy's own objective.** "Mana value deployed" is nearly
   `expensive-first`'s stated rule, *"spend the most mana available"*. A fitness function that is a
   competitor's objective cannot rank competitors. It was shipped as specified and disclosed rather
   than swapped, because replacing a metric in the sprint whose gate it failed is the one move
   pre-registration forbids.
2. **The solo objective cannot measure skill, and now needs to say what it *is* for.** A
   null-opponent model with a fixed horizon prices position at zero, so §5's gap decays to nothing
   as the horizon grows. §3 wants fitness and §5 wants a gap; 3.1 showed those want different
   instruments, and only the first of them belongs to a solo run.

## Exit criteria

- [x] An objective, **pre-registered with its degeneracy check**, and a written statement of what it
      is a score *of* and what it is *for*
- [x] Per-strategy scalarisation, with "max over admissible strategies" (§4) implemented rather than
      described
- [x] `mean + λ·CVaR₁₀`, sorted and reduced in fixed index order (§17.1)
- [~] Stratified openers under Neyman allocation (§7.3) — built, unbiased, **measured a loss**, not
      the default path
- [x] The solo gate wired as pass/fail into the fidelity ladder (§9)
- [~] §13.3's known-bad decks as a standing regression suite — four running, **two caught**
- [x] `make check` green at ≥95% line and branch

## Tasks

- [x] **T1 — Choose the objective.** `mf/objective`, and the answer is not the one the task
      anticipated.
      *The objective:* **total mana value cast in the aggregate phases** — turn 5 to the horizon,
      commander included, printed cost, lands zero. Three clauses are 3.1's score; the fourth is
      new and is a correction to it, because §3 says the opening contributes "zero to fitness
      beyond pass/fail" and 3.1's score summed the whole run. The design was right and the code
      had drifted.
      *The carried-in instruction could not be satisfied as written.* Every quantity the state
      vector records is some rung's greedy target — `deployed`→expensive-first, `spells`→
      cheapest-first, `mana`→ramp-first, `wasted`→careful lands, `mulligans`→the mulligan policy —
      because the rungs and the vector were derived from the same model of a turn. Looking for an
      objective no rung optimises is looking for a quantity the model cannot see.
      *So the check asks the real question:* does it rank decks, and does it rank strategies?
      **Condition 1 passes** (4.6x separation against a 0.20 bar). **Condition 2 fails**: `greedy`
      is the argmax on all four decks. Decomposed one parameter at a time, the mulligan is worth
      **+0.93 to +4.39** and the cast rule **−0.17 to −0.83** — opposite signs, and the worst rung
      wins *despite* its cast rule. Two corrections fall out: 3.1's disclosure named the wrong
      rung, and a first explanation of mine (the phase is a subset-sum so packing small must win)
      is refuted by a hand of `{2,3}` against a budget of 3. Both are now tests.
      The mirror is rejected on condition 1, as the calibrated example.
- [x] **T2 — Per-strategy scalarisation, and `max` over admissible strategies.** `mf_objective_fit`
      produces one row of §4's matrix and the max over it, with the band a bitmask so §5's
      per-bracket policy bands are a parameter rather than the whole ladder.
      The property that distinguishes a max from an average is tested as a property:
      **widening the band can never lower a deck's fitness.** An average falls the moment the
      added strategy is worse; a max cannot. An empty band yields the `MF_RUNG_COUNT` sentinel
      rather than fitness zero, which is a score a real deck could earn.
- [x] **T3 — CVaR₁₀ and `mean + λ·CVaR`.** λ = 0.5, the spec default, **left untuned on purpose**:
      λ trades ceiling against consistency and there is no ranking to judge the trade against until
      G4 in 3.3. Fitting a constant before there is anything to fit it to is how a metric acquires
      a value nobody can defend.
      *The sort is a counting sort, and that is a determinism decision before it is a speed one.* A
      comparison sort needs a tie-break rule to be reproducible and a rule nobody wrote down
      differs between partitions; counting never compares two elements, so the multiset determines
      the answer. Checked against an insertion sort that shares no code with it.
      The tail is `floor(n/10)` with a floor of one — **never more than a tenth**, since a CVaR
      over an eighth is a softer number wearing the same name.
      *Not by racing the deck against itself* (§13.6), and the argument stands.
      *Recorded:* the tail term does **not** rescue T1's strategy collapse. A loose mulligan should
      own the worst decile and be punished by it; the check ranks by the composite and `greedy` is
      still the argmax on all four decks.
- [x] **T4 — Stratified openers, Neyman allocation.** `mf/strata`, plus a conditional shuffle in
      `mf/deck` and the stratum threaded through `mf_phase_open_in` / `mf_solo_run_in`. Two-phase:
      a proportional pilot buys σ_h, Neyman spends the rest.
      **Measured, and §7.3's premise does not hold.** The opening-hand land count is *not* the
      dominant variance source: it accounts for **4.8%** of the variance of the objective, **7.2%**
      of the mana entering turn 5, and **3.2%** of the §3 gate. The best a perfect stratification
      on it could buy is **1.06x**, against §7.3's predicted 3–5x, and that ceiling holds at every
      horizon from one aggregate turn to eight.
      **End to end it is a loss** — variance ratio 0.5–0.8. Neyman needs σ_h, σ_h costs a pilot,
      and an allocation that varies run to run adds more variance than a 5% between-stratum share
      repays. Asserted as the loss it is; a test written to pass either way records nothing.
      **The reason is the mulligan, and §7.3 half saw it.** It notes the extreme strata "become
      mulligans" and treats that as the argument against truncating them — it is also why
      conditioning pays so little, since the policy discards and redraws exactly the hands the
      conditioning made unusual.
      *A defect the measurement found:* the first conditional shuffle biased the library *under*
      the hand. Taking "the first h lands" pushes whatever was over-represented early into the top
      of the remainder, so the deck was land-enriched exactly when the hand was conditioned to be
      land-poor. It moved the estimate by 0.4 mana and the agreement test's bar was wide enough to
      miss it — a bar that cannot fail is not a check. Both halves are now shuffled, and the
      residual composition is asserted directly rather than through a mean.
      **Kept but not the default path**, with the measurement as the deliverable.
- [x] **T5 — The solo gate in the fidelity ladder.** Pass rate counted beside the score, never
      folded into it. **A failed game does not score zero** — the tempting wiring multiplies, and
      that is exactly what §3 forbids, because it makes the turn-four board a fitness term through
      the back door and hardest on the archetypes the gate exists to protect. So the gate is per
      *deck* and the ranking does the ranking: `feasible` promotes, `fitness` orders.
      The bar is **0.5**, set from 2.3's measurement of real decks at 0.86/0.94 rather than
      guessed. A bar tight enough to bind on a real deck would be a second fitness term.
- [x] **T6 — §13.3's known-bad decks as a regression suite.** Four built and running in
      `make check`. **Two are caught and two are not, and that is the sprint's second finding.**
      | Deck | Fitness vs real | Gate | Caught? |
      | ---- | --------------- | ---- | ------- |
      | curve topping at 8, no ramp | 0.32x | 0.000 | yes |
      | five colours, bad mana base | 0.42x | 0.117 | yes |
      | 20 lands | 0.81x | 0.645 | **no** |
      | 60 lands | 0.94x | **0.914** | **no** |
      The two that are caught fail on *mana* — never enough, or never the right colours — which a
      solo model can see. The two that are not fail on **tempo**: flooding costs the turns spent
      drawing lands and screw costs the turns spent not casting, and a null-opponent model with a
      fixed horizon has no clock to charge them against. §13.3's own words are "if the simulator
      shrugs at the 20-land deck it is wrong, and you know before the GA ever runs" — it shrugs,
      and the 60-land deck clears the feasibility gate *more often than the real deck*.
      **Disclosed rather than patched**, on 3.1's precedent, and the numbers are asserted so a
      later fix fails here and forces the record to be updated.
      *Not delivered:* the `$30 vs $2,000 build` case, which needs prices the pipeline has but the
      metacard does not carry into a deck.
- [x] **T7 — Retro and amendments.** Twelve amendments across design §3, §4, §7.3, §9, §13.3
      and the spec, applied in the same change as the retro. `current_sprint` advanced to 3.3
      and 3.3 given its goal, exit criteria and open decisions — not task-split, which happens
      at its start.

## Decisions to make

| Decision | Status |
| -------- | ------ |
| What the objective measures | **Closed (T1).** Total mana value cast in the aggregate phases. "Not any policy's own rule" turned out to be unsatisfiable — every observable is one — and is replaced by a two-part check: does it rank decks, does it rank strategies |
| What a solo objective is *for*, given it cannot measure skill | **Constrained, not closed.** Sprint 5.0 now owns the policy gap and measures it in a mirror, so the solo objective is unambiguously **fitness for the GA** and nothing else — but T1 still has to say what fitness *measures*, and that is the sprint's work |
| λ in `mean + λ·CVaR₁₀` | **Closed at 0.5 (T3), and deliberately untuned.** The spec's default; there is no ranking to judge ceiling against consistency until G4, and fitting a constant to nothing is how a metric acquires a value nobody can defend |

## Risks carried in

- **Pre-registration does not protect against a degenerate metric** (3.1). It stops a metric being
  fitted to an answer; it does not stop one being a competitor's objective. Check explicitly.
- **A null-opponent model prices position at zero** (3.1). Any objective assuming otherwise will
  measure the opposite of what it intends — the mulligan component of §5's gap reverses sign at a
  12-turn horizon for exactly this reason.
- **The aggregation error is deck-differential** (3.1): +22% on high curves, −10% on artifact ramp.
  Every objective built on the aggregate phases inherits that bias, and **G4 in 3.3 cannot see it**.
- **A gate threshold in standard errors leaves the sample size blank** (2.3). Effect size beside
  significance, for G4.
- **The metacard carries no amounts** (2.2), and recurrence is now caught but amounts are not: draw
  and cost reduction still assume one.
