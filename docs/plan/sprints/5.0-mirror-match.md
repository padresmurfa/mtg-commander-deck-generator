# Sprint 5.0 — Mirror match

**Epic:** E5 Two-player · **Gates:** **G3** (re-run), **G1** (re-measured) · **Status:** `in_progress`

## Goal

**Give the model an opponent**, and re-run G3 against a mirror with its thresholds unchanged.

## Why now, ahead of E4

Pulled forward on the escalation from 3.3.1, answered across 3.3.2. **G4 failed** — the objective
ranks real decks at chance, ρ = −0.036 — and its branch says *redesign §3*. 3.3.2 then eliminated
the competing explanation: the model plays real precons perfectly well (median pass rate 0.400
against a bar mis-set at 0.5), so the problem is the objective and not basic playability.

That leaves **one explanation standing, and it has been found six times**:

| Where | What was found |
| ----- | -------------- |
| 3.1, G3 | §5's policy gap decays 19× from turn 4 to turn 12 |
| 3.2 T1 | The objective's strategy axis collapses onto `greedy` via the mulligan |
| 3.2 T4 | §7.3's stratification premise is false — the mulligan erases the conditioning variable |
| 3.2 T6 | Half of §13.3's known-bad decks escape, because flood and screw are *tempo* failures |
| 3.3.1, G4 | On 48 real decks, `greedy` wins 38 and `sequencing-aware` wins **none** |
| 3.3.2 | And it is not that the decks cannot be played |

**A null-opponent model with a fixed horizon prices position at zero.** Being a turn behind costs
nothing when nobody is racing you, so tempo — which is most of what §5's ladder expresses and most
of what separates real decks — has nowhere to show up in the score. Redesigning §3 against a model
that still cannot see position would be fitting a new objective to the same blindness.

E4 does not start first, for the third sprint running and now for a measured reason rather than a
precautionary one.

## What this sprint may not conclude

**A mirror's clock is self-scaled** — a slow deck races a slow deck. It is a *policy* instrument,
not a metagame, and it is the right one precisely because holding the deck fixed on both sides
removes every confound except the one under test. Whether §5's ladder separates against opponents
of *unrelated* speed is 5.1's question. Reading a narrow instrument as a wide result is 2.3's
mistake and the retro must not repeat it.

**It is also not a fitness axis.** A deck beats a copy of itself exactly half the time by symmetry
(§13.6), so the mirror ranks nothing. Everything here follows from the two sides being *identical*,
not from comparing them.

## The scope problem, stated before starting rather than discovered mid-sprint

The exit criterion says *"combat, life totals, removal resolution — sufficient for a game to end"*.
Those are not equal in cost:

- **Combat is free of the opcode set.** `mf_metacard` already carries `power` and `toughness`;
  attacking, blocking and damage need no new card vocabulary.
- **Removal is not.** There is no `MF_OP_DESTROY`, no damage, no bounce — the whole set is
  `TAP_FOR_MANA`, `ADD_MANA`, `ENTERS_TAPPED`, `DRAW`, `FETCH_LAND`, `COST_LESS`, plus `INERT` and
  `UNMATCHED`. Modelling removal means **growing the opcode set**, which is G1's own written failure
  branch, and it will move G1's number.

Both are in scope and neither is dropped. They are sequenced so combat lands first and a game can
end before removal is attempted — because a game that ends is what every other exit criterion
depends on, and a sprint that spends its first week on opcodes has nothing to test them with.

## Pre-registered, before any of it is built

**G3's prediction is already committed** (`gates.md`, before this sprint): §5's gap decays 19× from
turn 4 to turn 12 because being a turn behind costs nothing, so **against a mirror it must stop
decaying**. If it still decays, the tempo explanation is wrong and something else produced that
table. Thresholds unchanged: **5σ and 2×**. Components reported separately — the mulligan reversed
sign against a solo score, and this is where it reverses back or does not.

**And one more, committed now because it is the reason (b) was chosen over redesigning §3.** If the
absent opponent is what collapsed the strategy axis, then with a mirror in place:

> **`best_rung` must stop collapsing onto `greedy`.** In 3.3.1 it was greedy 38, curve-out 8,
> role-aware 2, sequencing-aware **0** across 48 real decks. Against a mirror, `sequencing-aware`
> must win on **more than zero** decks and `greedy`'s share must fall below the 79% it holds now.

This is a **prediction, not a gate** — it has no pass/fail consequence of its own. It is written
down so that "the opponent fixed it" cannot be asserted after the fact from a number that would have
moved anyway. If the collapse survives an opponent, the clock explanation is wrong after six
sightings, and that is a far more important finding than a gate result.

**Liveness is asserted before symmetry, and this is not an ordering preference.** A mirror in which
no game ever ends is perfectly symmetric, and a seat-swap check would pass on it. That is the 2.1
defect — a check reporting agreement it never observed — and the guard is that liveness is a
separate, earlier assertion with its own test.

## Exit criteria

- [ ] **Bit-exact seat-swap symmetry** — swapping the seats' RNG streams relabels the game log and

- [ ] **G3 re-run against a mirror, both thresholds unchanged**, verdict recorded as
      `passed` / `failed` / `deferred`
- [ ] Play/draw skew and self-clock recorded as **observables, scored by nothing**

## Tasks

- [x] **T1 — Pre-register.** Committed in `84b1e60`, before any interaction code existed.
- [x] **T2 — `mf/duel`: two seats and a life total.** Named *duel*, not *game*: `mf_game` is
      already the card set — paper, Arena, Magic Online. And not a new board either — `mf_board` is
      one seat's battlefield and `mf_live` one seat's whole live game, so two players is a pair of
      those plus life, a turn and a winner.
      **One stream per seat, derived in the module rather than at the call site.** Sharing one
      would deal both sides identical hands and the mirror would play itself in lockstep —
      perfectly symmetric, perfectly deterministic, and not a game. That failure is *invisible* to
      a seat-swap check, because a lockstep is symmetric. So the streams are tested for pairwise
      distinctness over a grid **and** for actually dealing different cards, which the numbers
      being unequal does not imply.
      `MF_DUEL_LIVE` is `0xFF` rather than a bool: "still running" and "seat 0 won" are different
      facts and a bool makes the second the default. A finished duel is **frozen, not sticky** —
      the first draft of that test expected damage to keep landing with the winner pinned, which
      leaves a final state depending on how much damage was in flight after the game ended.
      *A known omission, recorded rather than left to be noticed:* the 21-point commander-damage
      rule is not modelled. It needs to know which creature dealt which damage and combat here is
      aggregate. It shortens some real games and this model will not see it.
- [x] **T3 — Combat.** `mf/combat`, on the `power`/`toughness` already in `mf_metacard`. No new
      opcodes, which is why it comes before removal.
      **What is deliberately not modelled**, each of which shortens or lengthens real games:
      vigilance, trample, flying and all evasion, first strike, deathtouch, combat tricks, and
      commander damage. Everything that can attack does — a held-back blocker is a policy
      dimension §5's ladder should own, and inventing a rung mid-sprint would mean grading the
      ladder against a rung this sprint made up. It overstates aggressive decks, and the mirror
      cancels that only because both sides do it.
      **The block rule is a placeholder and says so:** a good block, or a chump block only against
      lethal. Defensible, not measured, and one fixed rule for both seats — which is what a mirror
      needs.
      *Mutation found a real defect.* The comment claimed the **smallest** good blocker takes the
      job; the loop took the **biggest**, and every test passed either way. It matters because
      attackers descend by *power* while a blocker needs *power* to beat *toughness* — a 5/1
      alongside a 3/9, against a 9/6 and a 2/6: spend the 9/6 on the 5/1 and the 3/9 walks in. The
      loop is fixed and that case is now the test. Six mutations, all caught; two of the first
      four were caught only by `-Werror`, so build-safe variants were run to confirm the *tests*
      catch them too.

## Risks carried in

- **Six sightings and one explanation is a comfortable story, and comfort is the risk.** The
  `best_rung` prediction exists to make it falsifiable rather than confirmable.
- **G4 is still failed and is not re-run here.** Nothing in this sprint un-fails it; the objective
  gets its verdict again only when something about the model has actually changed and a re-run has
  been pre-registered.
- **Every threshold set from a fixture is suspect** (3.3.2). G3's 5σ and 2× were set against
  hand-built decks and have never met a published list. They are unchanged here anyway — 3.1's rule
  — but the retro should say what they are worth.
- **The interaction layer is where a solo model's determinism guarantees are easiest to lose.**
  Two seats means two RNG streams, and reduction in completion order rather than seat order would
  break `determinism` silently.
