# Sprint 5.0 — Mirror match

**Epic:** E5 Two-player · **Gates:** **G3** (re-run), **G1** (re-measured) · **Status:** `in_progress`

## Goal

**Give the model an opponent**, and re-run G3 against a mirror with its thresholds unchanged.

## Why now, ahead of E4

Pulled forward on the escalation from 3.3.1, answered across 3.3.2. **G4 failed** — the objective
ranks real decks at chance, ρ = −0.036 — and its branch says *redesign §3*. 3.3.2 then eliminated
the competing explanation: the model plays real precons perfectly well (median pass rate 0.400
against a bar mis-set at 0.5), so the problem is the objective and not basic playability.

That leaves **one explanation standing, and it has been found six times**:

| Where | What was found |
| ----- | -------------- |
| 3.1, G3 | §5's policy gap decays 19× from turn 4 to turn 12 |
| 3.2 T1 | The objective's strategy axis collapses onto `greedy` via the mulligan |
| 3.2 T4 | §7.3's stratification premise is false — the mulligan erases the conditioning variable |
| 3.2 T6 | Half of §13.3's known-bad decks escape, because flood and screw are *tempo* failures |
| 3.3.1, G4 | On 48 real decks, `greedy` wins 38 and `sequencing-aware` wins **none** |
| 3.3.2 | And it is not that the decks cannot be played |

**A null-opponent model with a fixed horizon prices position at zero.** Being a turn behind costs
nothing when nobody is racing you, so tempo — which is most of what §5's ladder expresses and most
of what separates real decks — has nowhere to show up in the score. Redesigning §3 against a model
that still cannot see position would be fitting a new objective to the same blindness.

E4 does not start first, for the third sprint running and now for a measured reason rather than a
precautionary one.

## What this sprint may not conclude

**A mirror's clock is self-scaled** — a slow deck races a slow deck. It is a *policy* instrument,
not a metagame, and it is the right one precisely because holding the deck fixed on both sides
removes every confound except the one under test. Whether §5's ladder separates against opponents
of *unrelated* speed is 5.1's question. Reading a narrow instrument as a wide result is 2.3's
mistake and the retro must not repeat it.

**It is also not a fitness axis.** A deck beats a copy of itself exactly half the time by symmetry
(§13.6), so the mirror ranks nothing. Everything here follows from the two sides being *identical*,
not from comparing them.

## The scope problem, stated before starting rather than discovered mid-sprint

The exit criterion says *"combat, life totals, removal resolution — sufficient for a game to end"*.
Those are not equal in cost:

- **Combat is free of the opcode set.** `mf_metacard` already carries `power` and `toughness`;
  attacking, blocking and damage need no new card vocabulary.
- **Removal is not.** There is no `MF_OP_DESTROY`, no damage, no bounce — the whole set is
  `TAP_FOR_MANA`, `ADD_MANA`, `ENTERS_TAPPED`, `DRAW`, `FETCH_LAND`, `COST_LESS`, plus `INERT` and
  `UNMATCHED`. Modelling removal means **growing the opcode set**, which is G1's own written failure
  branch, and it will move G1's number.

Both are in scope and neither is dropped. They are sequenced so combat lands first and a game can
end before removal is attempted — because a game that ends is what every other exit criterion
depends on, and a sprint that spends its first week on opcodes has nothing to test them with.

## Pre-registered, before any of it is built

**G3's prediction is already committed** (`gates.md`, before this sprint): §5's gap decays 19× from
turn 4 to turn 12 because being a turn behind costs nothing, so **against a mirror it must stop
decaying**. If it still decays, the tempo explanation is wrong and something else produced that
table. Thresholds unchanged: **5σ and 2×**. Components reported separately — the mulligan reversed
sign against a solo score, and this is where it reverses back or does not.

**And one more, committed now because it is the reason (b) was chosen over redesigning §3.** If the
absent opponent is what collapsed the strategy axis, then with a mirror in place:

> **`best_rung` must stop collapsing onto `greedy`.** In 3.3.1 it was greedy 38, curve-out 8,
> role-aware 2, sequencing-aware **0** across 48 real decks. Against a mirror, `sequencing-aware`
> must win on **more than zero** decks and `greedy`'s share must fall below the 79% it holds now.

This is a **prediction, not a gate** — it has no pass/fail consequence of its own. It is written
down so that "the opponent fixed it" cannot be asserted after the fact from a number that would have
moved anyway. If the collapse survives an opponent, the clock explanation is wrong after six
sightings, and that is a far more important finding than a gate result.

**Liveness is asserted before symmetry, and this is not an ordering preference.** A mirror in which
no game ever ends is perfectly symmetric, and a seat-swap check would pass on it. That is the 2.1
defect — a check reporting agreement it never observed — and the guard is that liveness is a
separate, earlier assertion with its own test.

## Exit criteria

- [ ] Interaction layer — combat, life totals, removal resolution — sufficient for a game to end
- [ ] **Liveness asserted before symmetry**, so the symmetry check cannot pass vacuously
- [ ] **Bit-exact seat-swap symmetry** — swapping the seats' RNG streams relabels the game log and
      changes nothing else
- [ ] **G3 re-run against a mirror, both thresholds unchanged**, verdict recorded as
      `passed` / `failed` / `deferred`
- [ ] Play/draw skew and self-clock recorded as **observables, scored by nothing**
- [ ] **G1 re-measured** — 3.1 established it falls on reachability when an opponent arrives, and
      growing the opcode set for removal moves it too
- [ ] `make check` green at ≥95% line and branch

## Tasks

- [x] **T1 — Pre-register.** Committed in `84b1e60`, before any interaction code existed.
- [x] **T2 — `mf/duel`: two seats and a life total.** Named *duel*, not *game*: `mf_game` is
      already the card set — paper, Arena, Magic Online. And not a new board either — `mf_board` is
      one seat's battlefield and `mf_live` one seat's whole live game, so two players is a pair of
      those plus life, a turn and a winner.
      **One stream per seat, derived in the module rather than at the call site.** Sharing one
      would deal both sides identical hands and the mirror would play itself in lockstep —
      perfectly symmetric, perfectly deterministic, and not a game. That failure is *invisible* to
      a seat-swap check, because a lockstep is symmetric. So the streams are tested for pairwise
      distinctness over a grid **and** for actually dealing different cards, which the numbers
      being unequal does not imply.
      `MF_DUEL_LIVE` is `0xFF` rather than a bool: "still running" and "seat 0 won" are different
      facts and a bool makes the second the default. A finished duel is **frozen, not sticky** —
      the first draft of that test expected damage to keep landing with the winner pinned, which
      leaves a final state depending on how much damage was in flight after the game ended.
      *A known omission, recorded rather than left to be noticed:* the 21-point commander-damage
      rule is not modelled. It needs to know which creature dealt which damage and combat here is
      aggregate. It shortens some real games and this model will not see it.
- [x] **T3 — Combat.** `mf/combat`, on the `power`/`toughness` already in `mf_metacard`. No new
      opcodes, which is why it comes before removal.
      **What is deliberately not modelled**, each of which shortens or lengthens real games:
      vigilance, trample, flying and all evasion, first strike, deathtouch, combat tricks, and
      commander damage. Everything that can attack does — a held-back blocker is a policy
      dimension §5's ladder should own, and inventing a rung mid-sprint would mean grading the
      ladder against a rung this sprint made up. It overstates aggressive decks, and the mirror
      cancels that only because both sides do it.
      **The block rule is a placeholder and says so:** a good block, or a chump block only against
      lethal. Defensible, not measured, and one fixed rule for both seats — which is what a mirror
      needs.
      *Mutation found a real defect.* The comment claimed the **smallest** good blocker takes the
      job; the loop took the **biggest**, and every test passed either way. It matters because
      attackers descend by *power* while a blocker needs *power* to beat *toughness* — a 5/1
      alongside a 3/9, against a 9/6 and a 2/6: spend the 9/6 on the 5/1 and the 3/9 walks in. The
      loop is fixed and that case is now the test. Six mutations, all caught; two of the first
      four were caught only by `-Werror`, so build-safe variants were run to confirm the *tests*
      catch them too.
- [x] **T4 — The game loop, and a game that ends.** Alternating turns to a win or a turn cap.
      **§3's aggregation cannot host an opponent, and this was found on starting rather than
      planned for.** An aggregate phase has no turns — that is the whole of what aggregating
      means — and combat is a per-turn exchange between two boards. So a duel is *exact for its
      whole length*, and §3's exact-early/aggregate-late split describes the solo model only. The
      clock is precisely the order-dependent thing §3 assumed away, so an opponent removes the
      assumption rather than stressing it. Two consequences, both load-bearing:
      **the duel's turn and the opening phase's turn have to be the same code** — `mf_turn_play`
      was extracted from `mf_phase_play_live`, which is now a loop over it, because this sprint
      exists to measure what an opponent changes and a second copy of the turn would put a
      modelling difference inside the measurement; and **T8 must compare against an *exact* solo
      baseline** at the same horizon, or it will attribute 3.1's aggregation error to the opponent.
      `mf_turn_policy.turns` is a parameter, so that baseline is free.
      The extraction is behaviour-preserving and the **golden run digests are the evidence** — a
      refactor with no new behaviour of its own has nothing else to show.
      **Twenty turns per seat**, argued before it was measured: forty life, no evasion, and damage
      that gets through only when the attack outclasses what can block it make this game slower
      than real Magic rather than faster, so a cap has to sit well past the turn 8–15 a real
      Commander game is decided on or the *cap* decides the game rather than the deck. T5 measures
      the rate at which games actually end inside it.
      `first` says which seat is on the play — it takes the first turn and skips the draw on it,
      the only asymmetry between two identical decks. T6 adds the stream swap alongside it, because
      §13.6's symmetry check is **paired** and a pure relabelling moves the play as well as the
      cards.
      **29 mutations, all caught** — 17 on `mf/duel`, 12 on the extracted turn — and three of them
      found something. The seats' per-ply *policy* was unpinned: a loop reading `p[0]` for both
      survived a greedy-versus-curve-out check, because those two rungs also mulligan differently
      and what the check saw was the opening hand, not the play; the test now adds `role-aware`
      against `sequencing-aware`, whose mulligans are identical field for field. The *fixture*
      could not tell the rungs apart at all at first — one land type, one cost, a hand every policy
      keeps — and even after a curve was added, eight taplands left the two land rules playing an
      identical game in three quarters of the sample. And the defender's real life total reaching
      `mf_combat` was unpinned: feeding a constant 40 left every test green, because chump blocks
      fire in about one attack in twenty-six and none of the individually named games was one.
      Separately, removing the commander guard from the cast loop does not *fail* — it does not
      *terminate*, which is that loop's own written termination argument arriving as a measurement.
      **An assumption corrected by measuring it.** Everything that can attack does, and attacking
      taps, so the defender's board looked like it would always be tapped out and blocking looked
      nearly impossible — which was very nearly written down as a finding. Measured instead: a
      blocker is available in **88.1%** of attacks and something blocks in **26.2%**, because
      creatures cast on the defender's own turn are summoning-sick, and sickness stops a creature
      attacking and not blocking.
- [x] **T5 — Liveness, asserted first.** Games end, someone reaches zero life, inside the cap, and
      the *rate* at which they do is a number rather than a yes.
      `mf_duel_sample_live` is §13.6's three conditions as an AND: games ended, the length
      distribution has spread, and the winner is not always the same seat. **Scoped to a mirror**,
      and deliberately — T8 fixes the better rung in seat 0, and it winning every game is a live
      sample rather than a dead one, so the third condition is not valid in general and narrowing
      the predicate is cheaper than weakening it.
      **The guard on the guard.** A liveness check that cannot report a *dead* sample is the
      vacuous pass it exists to prevent, one level up — 2.1's defect reintroduced. The stall
      fixture is what proves it can say no: two boards of 0/4s attacking each other for twenty
      turns apiece, perfectly symmetric and perfectly dead.
      **Measured on the real corpus, not on a fixture built to end** — a liveness rate taken only
      from decks chosen to kill is close to vacuous. 188 of 190 precons buildable, on the same card
      table 3.3.1's G4 used (`349888c09657e4361deac00e01c0f21c`), 256 games per deck:

      | Rung | End rate | Decks live | Mean plies | Per-deck rate |
      | ---- | -------: | ---------: | ---------: | ------------- |
      | `greedy` | 0.8938 | 188/188 | 26.79 | [0.340, 1.000] |
      | `curve-out` | 0.8826 | 188/188 | 27.08 | [0.266, 1.000] |
      | `role-aware` | 0.8856 | 188/188 | 26.98 | [0.281, 1.000] |
      | `sequencing-aware` | 0.8855 | 188/188 | 26.80 | [0.273, 1.000] |

      **The cap binds on about one game in nine.** Recorded rather than fixed: 20 was argued in T4
      before any of this was run, and moving it now would be choosing a horizon from the
      measurement it is supposed to be independent of. **T8 has to account for the censoring** — a
      win-rate statistic over a sample where 11% of games have no winner is not a win rate unless
      the unended games are handled explicitly.
      **And the self-clock is a lower bound.** The mean is over the games that *ended*, so it is
      biased downward: the longest games are exactly the ones the cap censored. The true figure is
      above 13.5 turns per seat, which makes §3's solo horizon of 12 if anything *too short* — the
      calibration §13.6 said a mirror could supply, arriving with the sign nobody predicted.
      Provisional in any case: no removal yet (T7), a placeholder block rule, no evasion.
      **17 mutations, all caught** — four of them only after build-safe variants, because they died
      at `-Werror` on an unused parameter and a compiler rejection says nothing about the tests.
      A third fixture was needed: a deck that kills its own copy *sometimes* (0.42), because an end
      rate of 1.0 and one of 0.0 both exercise a rate as a flag, and the mean and spread are only
      interesting over a mixed sample.
- [x] **T6 — Seat-swap symmetry, bit-exact.** And the half-of-the-time identity §13.6 promises,
      which is the two-player model's only analytic ground truth.
      **A relabelling moves three things, not one.** §13.6 states the check as *the same game with
      the two players' names exchanged*, and a player is a stream, a policy, **and** whether it is
      on the play. Swapping only the streams gives a different game rather than the same one
      renamed — so the seating is one struct (`mf_seating`) the module can flip whole, rather than
      two booleans a caller can flip half of, and `mf_seating_mirror` is an involution asserted
      rather than assumed.
      `mf_duel_symmetric` is the check: run the game, run its relabelling, require equal length,
      permuted winner and active seat, and **both seats byte-for-byte identical the other way
      round**. Two games, not a hundred thousand — the statistical form sees only asymmetries that
      survive averaging.
      **Liveness is asserted before it, and there is a test saying why.** The stall fixture is
      perfectly symmetric and perfectly dead: `symmetry_holds_on_games_that_never_end_and_proves_
      nothing_there` passes the symmetry check and then fails `mf_duel_sample_live` on the next
      line, so "symmetry passes" can never be read as "the engine works".
      **The half-the-time identity comes out exact.** `mf_duel_paired_sample` runs each game with
      its relabelling; the two halves have the same length and opposite winners, so each seat wins
      *precisely* half of what ended and the ended count is even. Not 0.5 ± noise — 0.5. It holds
      for a **lopsided matchup** too (greedy against sequencing-aware), because it is a statement
      about seats and not about decks, which is what makes a paired sample the right shape for T8.
      The test contrasts it against an unpaired sample of the same size, where the seats do *not*
      split evenly, so what pairing bought is visible rather than asserted.
      **A process note, recorded rather than glossed.** T6's tests were written before the code but
      never observed failing: the functions did not exist, so the suite was a compile error rather
      than a red assertion. Mutation testing is the substitute CLAUDE.md §1 names for exactly this,
      and it is what the confidence here rests on — and it earned its keep immediately.
      **19 mutations, and five survived the first pass: every one that made the check *weaker*.**
      Including replacing the whole comparison with `return true`. Mutations that made it
      *stricter* were all caught, and mutations that made it laxer were not — which is precisely
      the wrong way round, and the same defect as 2.1: a checker with no failing case reports
      agreement it never observed. The cause was that every test asserted only that symmetry
      *held*, so nothing could distinguish a working comparison from a constant.
      The fix is a pure predicate, `mf_duel_relabels(a, b)`, split out of the runner so it can be
      fed pairs that are *not* relabellings and required to say no — a game against itself, a
      different game, and one perturbation per branch. All nineteen are caught now. **A symmetry
      check needs a case where it fails, and this one shipped without one.**
      **The suite got too slow and was cut back.** The first version sampled 512 games in several
      tests, which pushed `make check` past twenty minutes — almost all of it the ASan build, which
      runs the whole suite at `-O0` under two sanitizers. Measured at 64, 128 and 192 games, every
      assertion holds identically (the identity is exact at any size), so the samples were cut
      about fourfold and re-pinned. A suite nobody will wait for is a suite that stops being run.
- [x] **T7 — Removal, and the opcode set grows.** G1's failure branch, taken deliberately for the
      first time — and it went much further than "grow the set".
      **G1 went stale in T4, when combat landed, not in T7.** The classifier's step 1 returned
      *inert* for attack, block and death triggers on an argument written into the module: "§3 does
      not simulate combat, so an attack trigger never fires... nothing dies in a phase where
      nothing attacks." A duel makes every one of those events happen. §8's own sentence — *"a
      later sprint that widens the simulated phases must re-measure; inertia is relative to what is
      being simulated, and that changes"* — came due.
      Those clauses are now **reachable and not fired**: the duel resolves combat and does not run
      triggered abilities, so they are unmatched rather than classified by shape. Classifying by
      shape would be worse than either — "When this creature dies, draw a card" would be recorded
      as a card the deck draws.
      **The wide reading, chosen by the owner and taken deliberately.** A duel resolves combat on
      power and toughness and tracks life totals, so anything changing who can attack, who can
      block, how big a creature is, whether it survives, or what a life total is, is text the model
      *would* observe and cannot express. The narrow alternative — keywords stay inert because this
      combat never *consults* them — was available and rejected: it lets a model call itself
      faithful by declining to look.
      **Two new opcodes**, `MF_OP_DESTROY` and `MF_OP_DAMAGE`, and the metacard's `reserved` byte
      spent on the printed damage amount. That is a real change to the equivalence classes — two
      burn spells differing only in how much they deal used to be one class and are now two, which
      is correct, because a 2-damage spell and a 7-damage spell kill different creatures.
      Removal resolves in the duel, before combat: `mf_turn_play` *reports* what was cast and
      `mf_duel` decides what it hits, exactly as `mf_combat` hands back damage. The turn keeps no
      opponent, which is what lets the solo phase share it. Damage kills the biggest creature it
      can reach or goes to the face — **a placeholder, on the T3 precedent**, one fixed rule for
      both seats.

      | -- | ----: | ----------------------: |
      | Before (3.1) | 0.9301 | — |
      | T7, removal verbs matched bare | 0.2252 | 7,167 / 31,829 |

      Clause histogram: `destroy` 189, `damage` 803, inert 34,991, **unmatched 36,821** — unmatched
      now exceeds inert, which is the whole shape of the finding.
      **The instrument is coarse and the bias runs both ways.** The keyword list is not exhaustive,
      which overstates G1; substring matching catches text it should not ("reach" also matches
      "reaches"), which understates it. Measured once: qualifying the removal verbs by what is
      being removed — rather than matching `destroy`/`exile`/`sacrifice` bare, which also caught
      library effects and mana abilities — was worth about four points. A more careful sweep would
      be worth more. The number is a measurement of a coarse instrument and is recorded as one.
      **T10 records the verdict.** G1 is not adjudicated here.

## Risks carried in

- **Six sightings and one explanation is a comfortable story, and comfort is the risk.** The
  `best_rung` prediction exists to make it falsifiable rather than confirmable.
- **G4 is still failed and is not re-run here.** Nothing in this sprint un-fails it; the objective
  gets its verdict again only when something about the model has actually changed and a re-run has
  been pre-registered.
- **Every threshold set from a fixture is suspect** (3.3.2). G3's 5σ and 2× were set against
  hand-built decks and have never met a published list. They are unchanged here anyway — 3.1's rule
  — but the retro should say what they are worth.
- **The interaction layer is where a solo model's determinism guarantees are easiest to lose.**
  Two seats means two RNG streams, and reduction in completion order rather than seat order would
  break `determinism` silently.
